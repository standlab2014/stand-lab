Research

The questions
we ask

Alzheimer's disease begins two decades before the first symptom. Our work is about reading that silent interval.

Amyloid gathers, tau spreads through connected brain regions, and cognition holds steady — until it does not. We work at the point where imaging, blood tests and the clinic meet: a tau PET scan can now stage the disease in a living person, and a blood draw can flag the same pathology at a fraction of the cost. Neither is useful until we know exactly what its numbers mean for the patient in front of us.

Our work takes four forms:

  • Research — biomarker studies in deeply characterised cohorts, from PET imaging and plasma assays through to nationwide population data.
  • Clinical care — a memory clinic for early-onset dementia, mild cognitive impairment and subjective cognitive decline, including counselling on new disease-modifying therapies.
  • Collaboration — international consortia including LEADS and multicentre tau PET tracer comparisons, alongside partners at UCSF and across Korea.
  • Teaching — training residents, fellows and graduate students, and serving on the education and academic committees of the Korean Dementia Association.

Four lines of work

01

Tau and amyloid PET

Staging Alzheimer's pathology in vivo: how tau PET predicts who converts from mild cognitive impairment to dementia, how visual reads compare with quantification, and how different tracers agree across centres.

02

Blood-based biomarkers

Plasma p-tau217 and related markers as a scalable first line — detecting Alzheimer neuropathology across clinical syndromes, and predicting the pathological stage that PET would show.

03

Risk, prevention and brain health

What moves the curve: physical activity, sleep, blood pressure, diabetes and metabolic health, studied in both biomarker cohorts and nationwide population data covering millions of people.

04

How tau spreads

Network models of tau propagation, the interaction between amyloid and tau that accelerates it, and how the pattern differs between early- and late-onset disease.
